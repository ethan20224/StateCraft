**Short answer:** stop trying to win the AI race as a runner. Make Singapore the place the race can't route around and can't distrust, and the country that turns AI into higher wages fastest. The first half is urgent. As of last week, Singapore's problem isn't irrelevance. It's being seen as the place where chips leak.

I've written the full strategy as a dossier: [dossier-singapore-national-ai-strategy-2026-11-25.md](dossier-singapore-national-ai-strategy-2026-11-25.md). It has the power map, the options scored against each other, a wargame, a pre-mortem, a dashboard with tripwires, and a week-by-week first 100 days. Below is the core of it. Since you couldn't weigh in, I've assumed you're in the Prime Minister's seat (he chairs the National AI Council) and working towards the election due by 2030.

## First, two problems with the question

**"Matter in the AI race" assumes there's one race and Singapore is in it.** There isn't, and PM Wong has already said publicly, correctly, that ["our advantage does not lie in building the largest frontier models."](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366638920/Singapore-to-form-National-AI-Council-expands-tax-breaks-to-ease-AI-adoption) Singapore already matters more than most people realise:

- It makes about [one in ten of the world's chips and a fifth of chipmaking equipment](https://www.edb.gov.sg/en/news-and-insights/singapore-announces-sg-semiconductor-to-strengthen-its-role-in-the-global-semiconductor-ecosystem).
- Micron's [first HBM packaging plant outside Taiwan](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/11/07/micron-strengthens-singapore-footprint-with-us24bil-investment-in-ai-driven-manufacturing) starts output in early 2027.
- OpenAI and Google DeepMind run applied labs here.
- GIC [co-led a US$65bn frontier-lab round](https://technode.global/2026/05/29/gic-temasek-anchor-singapores-investment-in-anthropics-65b-series-h-round/).
- Global AI spending is the main reason the [2026 growth forecast was raised to 4.5–5.5%](https://www.meti.gov.sg/newsroom/mti-upgrades-2026-gdp-growth-forecast-to--4-5-to-5-5-per-cent-/).

**"Just another government PDF" is also a bit unfair.** The strategy [refreshed in May](https://mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/update-to-singapore-s-national-ai-strategy--refreshed-priorities-to-harness-ai-for-the-public-good-factsheet) isn't dumb. Its real problems are that it has ten priorities, which in practice means none, and that it was written before the ground moved.

## What changed on 16 November

Washington put Singapore on a [licence list for advanced AI chips](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/international-compliance-blog/bis-targets-data-center-remote-access-and-imposes-steeper-requirements-on-foundries-and-osats.html), alongside Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil, as a higher-than-normal diversion risk. The key details:

- Licences are decided case by case. One stated factor is how effective Singapore's own export-control regime is.
- US-headquartered firms are exempt. Singapore-headquartered firms are not.
- Existing data centres must be licensed by [17 February 2027](https://hlc.com/en/publications/bis-issues-new-controls-targeting-remote-access-to-advanced-us-computing-power).

The trigger was the US criminal probe into [Megaspeed](https://fortune.com/2026/11/17/sg-us-ai-chip-controls-southeast-asia/), a Singapore-based Nvidia buyer accused of diverting billions of dollars of chips to China. Megaspeed denies it.

Singapore's [official position](https://www.meti.gov.sg/newsroom/oral-reply-to-pqs-on-singapore-s-approach-to-export-controls/) has been that it has no obligation to enforce other countries' one-sided export controls. That stance worked when the world was globalising. Now that the US is choking off access to key technologies, it reads as tolerance. Notice too that eleven months ago Singapore was the [only Southeast Asian founding member of Pax Silica](https://fortune.com/2025/12/30/why-singapore-is-the-only-southeast-asian-country-in-pax-silica-ai/), the US-led club for trusted AI supply chains. Membership didn't protect us. Our enforcement record decided it.

Frontier AI models are now a choke point too. In June, a US directive [cut foreign nationals off Anthropic's newest models](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/6/14/us-orders-anthropic-to-disable-ai-models-for-all-foreign-nationals), even though GIC is an investor. Owning a stake doesn't buy access. Access is decided between governments.

So the thing actually holding Singapore back right now is permission from Washington. It isn't money, talent or even energy, at least not yet. A policy has to pass four gates: it has to work, pass, be delivered and last. This is the "Passes" gate, which means getting the authority to act. For a small open economy, that authority sits partly outside the country.

## The strategy: Trusted Node, Fastest Adopter

The organising rule: **line up with the US on the few choke-point technologies, and stay open on everything else.** That means advanced chips and the computing power they provide. On models, talent, trade, capital and Chinese firms' regional headquarters, Singapore stays open to all lawful business. There are five moves.

**1. Trusted compute (now to 12 months).** Don't write a new strategy. Amend the [Digital Infrastructure Bill](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/new-digital-infrastructure-bill-to-strengthen-the-foundations-for-singapore-s-digital-economy/), which is already in Parliament and already licenses large data centres and cloud providers. Add:
- know-your-customer rules for compute buyers, down to the ultimate parent company
- a register of every AI accelerator, checked against Customs import records
- audit and inspection powers, a duty to report suspected diversion, and real penalties

This is Singapore law protecting Singapore's reputation, applied the same way to every nationality. The Netherlands did exactly this with ASML's machines in 2023: it wrote its own export rules instead of applying America's.

Then take the regime to Washington and ask for what the UAE got in July: an [approved-entity list](https://www.morganlewis.com/ru/pubs/2026/07/bis-upgrades-uae-export-control-status-with-ai-chip-access-limited-to-approved-entities) giving named organisations licence-free access. For Singapore that list would cover government agencies, named Singapore operators and US cloud providers' local subsidiaries. Ask Nvidia, the big cloud providers and Micron, all of whom have a lot riding on Singapore, to file supporting comments before the 18 January deadline.

**2. Hardware the world can't do without.** Don't build a leading-edge chip fab. Put the money where AI hardware is scarce and the US needs Singapore: HBM memory and advanced packaging, silicon photonics, power electronics and chipmaking equipment. Tie every incentive to output targets, with clawbacks if firms miss them. Build the technician pipeline, because the US CHIPS Act showed that workforce, not money, is what holds fabs back.

**3. Power priced by value.** Energy is the hard ceiling:
- [Up to 95% of electricity comes from gas.](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/business/economy/2026/10/1550070/singapore-set-miss-2035-green-power-import-target)
- Data centres already use [more than 7% of the grid](https://www.mallesons.com/content/mallesons/sg/en/insights/latest-thinking/singapore-launches-200mw-data-centre-call-for-application-dc-cfa2).
- Wood Mackenzie expects imported clean power to reach about half the 2035 target. None of the 9.25GW of approved import projects has reached financial close.

So reserve local capacity for what earns its megawatts: AI inference, regulated finance, health and government workloads, and a sovereign fallback. Make data centres pay the full cost of new supply, and cap their share of the grid at roughly 12% by 2030.

Push bulk computing to a Johor ring under a shared verification standard. Then use the 2027 ASEAN chairmanship to propose that standard across the region. That gives our neighbours a route off the same licence list, and gives us an overflow ring we can trust.

On nuclear, hold the IAEA review in 2027 and commit to deciding by 2029. Reactors take a decade or more to build, so deciding late is the same as deciding no.

**4. Spreading AI through the economy is the main event.** Only [23% of SMEs use AI, against 70% of large firms](https://sea.peoplemattersglobal.com/news/ai-and-emerging-tech/singapore-ai-adoption-rises-to-235percent-as-digital-economy-reaches-dollar1441bn-52560). Retrenchments are at their [highest since 2023](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/06/15/more-retrenchments-in-singapore-in-q1-2026-as-job-vacancies-drop). What to do:
- Set up a roughly 20-person AI Delivery Unit in the Prime Minister's Office. Give it five priorities and a monthly review with the PM.
- Give each National AI Mission one owner and a public target for output, not for adoption rates. Count tool usage and you'll get tool usage, not productivity.
- For SMEs, pay implementation partners for results delivered, and build shared AI tools for each sector once rather than funding thousands of separate efforts.
- Build a sovereign fallback: the best open-weight models running on Singapore's own computers, with a drill every quarter simulating a foreign cut-off.
- Do not build a national frontier model. The national supercomputer has [1,536 GPUs](https://www.nscc.sg/portfolio/item/nsccs-aspire-2b-debuts-at-rank-44-and-184-on-top500-list/). Frontier labs train on hundreds of thousands.

**5. Talent the public will accept.** The new [ONE Pass AI and Tech track](https://singaporeglobalnetwork.gov.sg/stories/business/explained-singapores-2026-global-talent-policy-changes-and-why-it-works-in-favour-of-top-talent/) launches in January. Use it actively: recruit 500 to 1,000 top-tier people by 2029. Make every lab and anchor-investor deal include commitments to train Singaporeans. Publish Singaporeans' share of AI jobs and wages every year. Pilot wage insurance through the Tripartite Jobs Council, topping up pay for workers displaced by AI who take lower-paid jobs. Openness only survives if people can see it paying them.

## Practical steps: the first 100 days

- **This week:** Emergency session of the National AI Council. Name one accountable minister and a special envoy to Washington. Announce the compute-integrity amendments and a joint enforcement taskforce (Customs, IMDA, CSA, the Commercial Affairs Department, MAS). Brief Beijing and Kuala Lumpur privately before the announcement, not after.
- **December:** Count every AI accelerator in licensed data centres, with a voluntary-disclosure window. Run a licensing clinic, Singapore-headquartered operators first. Handle Megaspeed quickly and visibly, within due process, the way MAS handled 1MDB.
- **By 18 January:** File formal comments on the US rule, and put forward a government-to-government proposal for a Trusted Compute Arrangement.
- **Late January:** Second Reading of the Bill. ONE Pass recruitment begins. As ASEAN chair, table a compute-integrity workstream.
- **February:** Budget 2027 funds the Delivery Unit, pilots for SME implementation partners, guarantees to unblock power imports, nuclear groundwork and the wage-insurance pilot. Publish five priorities with metrics, replacing the ten.
- **By 17 February:** Every existing data-centre operator has filed for its licence.
- **March:** First Delivery Unit review with the PM. First sovereign cut-off drill. Nuclear decision timeline published.

## The uncomfortable part

The hardest call is one I can't make for you. Siding with the US on compute means some Chinese AI workloads will leave Singapore. Beijing will be irritated. And we'd be accepting a rule that openly favours American firms over Singaporean ones.

The strongest case against is "we're outsourcing our sovereignty to Washington's export bureaucracy, for chips we might be able to buy elsewhere in five years." That isn't a silly argument. I still recommend siding with the US on this, for three reasons:
- No other supplier can provide leading-edge AI chips at scale.
- Singapore's semiconductor industry already sits inside the American ecosystem.
- A downgrade, which could mean Entity List designations and import restrictions, would be far worse than irritating Beijing over a narrow technical rule that applies to everyone equally.

Keep the alignment technical and never rhetorical, and keep the door wide open to China everywhere else.

Also build for American unreliability. In 22 months Washington has issued a sweeping chip rule, scrapped it, opened a taxed channel for H200 sales to China, and then tightened on Southeast Asia. Being verifiable pays off either way: if the US tightens, Singapore is already ahead; if it loosens, the reputation for trust still sells.

**Odds:** My judgement is about 15% that Singapore hits most of its 2030 targets on the current path. With this design, about 30%. For trusted access alone, about 65–70% by the end of 2027. The low overall number reflects how demanding the full target set is, not a fragile plan. Washington's permission is the main constraint now. From 2028 it shifts to delivery: getting SMEs to adopt AI and getting enough power.

**Statecraft lesson.** In a world where great powers control choke points, small states don't stay safe by being neutral. They stay safe by being indispensable and verifiable. Neutrality is a posture great powers can ignore; verifiability is an asset they have to price in. Switzerland learned this between 2009 and 2017: bank secrecy collapsed under US pressure, and the banking centre survived only by becoming auditable. Singapore's own financial centre learned it in 2016, when MAS shut down BSI Bank. Singapore lost a bank and gained a reputation.

**A question for you:** if you had to choose, which can Singapore least afford to lose, Washington's chips or Beijing's goodwill? And does your answer change if the US reverses itself again in 2029?
